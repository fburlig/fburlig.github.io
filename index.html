Fiona Burlig is an associate professor at the Harris School of Public Policy at the University of Chicago.

research

I'm an environmental and energy economist. I study how households, firms, and governments respond to environmental change and environmental policy, with a particular focus on energy, water, and climate adaptation in low- and middle-income countries.

Some current work…

  • Paying for power · with Anant Sudarshan
  • Insurance and the demand for adaptation: Theory and experimental evidence from West Bengal · with Manzoor Dar, Xavier Giné, Erin Kelley, and Gregory Lane
  • The value of clean water: Experimental evidence from rural India · with Amir Jina and Anant Sudarshan · American Economic Review
  • Groundwater and crop choice in the short and long run · with Louis Preonas and Matt Woerman · Revise and resubmit, Review of Economic Studies
  • Beliefs, forecasts, and investments: Experimental evidence from India · with Amir Jina, Erin Kelley, Gregory Lane, and Harshil Sahai · Revise and resubmit, American Economic Journal: Applied Economics

me

Before Chicago I was a postdoc in the economics department here, and before that I did a PhD in agricultural and resource economics at Berkeley and a BA at Williams. I'm a faculty research fellow at NBER, an affiliate of J-PAL, BREAD, and the IGC, and deputy faculty director of EPIC-India and the Odisha Data, Policy, and Innovation Centre.
